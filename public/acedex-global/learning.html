Learning

Learning that grows with you.

Knowledge, curiosity and character belong together. Explore the principles behind the AIS learning journey.

Understanding comes first.

Strong foundations grow from clear explanations, meaningful practice and feedback that helps a learner take the next step. We want children to connect ideas, explain their thinking and use knowledge in real situations.

A journey through school.

The planned AIS journey spans Early Years, Primary and Secondary education. Campus offerings, entry classes and curriculum arrangements are local decisions and must be confirmed with the school.

Early Years

Early learning begins with secure relationships, language, movement and play. A thoughtful environment encourages children to explore, communicate, cooperate and become comfortable with new experiences.

Primary School

Primary education develops literacy, numeracy and a broad understanding of the world. Reading, purposeful questioning and guided practice help children build competence and confidence together.

Secondary School

Secondary learning deepens subject knowledge and introduces greater responsibility for study. Practical tasks, discussion and independent enquiry help learners develop judgement and prepare for further learning.

Teaching that makes learning visible.

Learning goals, well chosen examples and regular opportunities to practise help students understand what progress looks like. Feedback should identify what is working and make the next improvement achievable.

Technology serves the learning purpose. It should support exploration, creativity and responsible participation while preserving the value of discussion, reading and hands on work.

Belonging supports progress.

Children need to feel respected, heard and supported. Our vision connects academic learning with wellbeing, creative interests, physical activity and positive relationships.

Families are partners in that journey. Each campus will provide its confirmed routes for communication, pastoral support and safeguarding concerns.
